# 🚛 RMC Transit Mixer Fleet Operations Analytics

> RMC fleet operations analytics covering driver TAT analysis, shift performance, raw material prediction and RFM-based customer classification — built on real operational data from a Telangana-based RMC operator.

---

## 🏗️ Project Overview

Ready Mix Concrete (RMC) fleet operations involve multiple transit mixer trucks making repeated trips between a batching plant and construction sites throughout the day. Managing driver performance, ensuring timely deliveries, predicting raw material requirements and understanding client behaviour are critical challenges for fleet operators.

This project analyses daily trip log data from a real RMC fleet operator  — providing actionable insights for operations managers through three layers of analysis: operations, prediction and customer classification.

---

## 📊 Features

### ⏱️ TAT Analysis & Prediction
- Shift-wise TAT patterns (Early Morning / Morning / Afternoon / Evening / Night)
- TAT prediction based on historical patterns


### 🧱 Raw Material Prediction
- Cement consumption estimation
- Sand requirement forecasting
- Daily and weekly demand projection

### 🏢 RFM Customer Classification
Clients are classified using the **RFM Model**:

| Dimension | What It Measures |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Recency** | How recently the client placed an order |
| **Frequency** | How often the client orders |
| **Monetary** | Revenue generated by the client |

This classifies construction clients into tiers — helping the operator prioritise high-value repeat clients and plan fleet allocation accordingly.

## 📈 Key Findings & Recommendations

### 🧱 4.1 Raw Material Consumption Forecasting

#### Interpretation
- Daily cement and sand consumption is highly variable with no strong linear trend
- Regression R² is low (0.1921), meaning usage depends on changing factors like order patterns and unexpected surges
- Sand demand is scaled 2.42x above cement due to mix ratio, reflecting the actual physical relationship in usage

#### Recommendations
- **Plan with Safety Stock** — Always procure above forecasted amounts to avoid unexpected shortages
- **Update Forecasts Weekly** — Adjust next-week predictions using latest moving averages on cleaned data
- **Monitor for Spikes** — Watch for sudden surges due to special client demands and adjust estimates quickly

---

### ⏱️ 4.2 Turnaround Time (TAT) Analysis

#### Interpretation
- TAT is significantly lower during early morning and night hours — afternoon and evening slots show the highest delays
- Traffic congestion is confirmed as the primary cause of delivery delays
- Performance varies across drivers — some handle more trips at higher efficiency while others show consistently higher turnaround times

#### Recommendations
- **Schedule During Low-Traffic Windows** — Prioritise deliveries between 3–7 AM and 9–11 PM where TAT is lowest
- **Driver Performance Programs** — Train high-TAT drivers on efficient driving and route selection
- **Match Routes to Driver Strengths** — Assign longer routes to drivers proven to handle them efficiently
- **Balance Trip Allocation** — Ensure fair and optimised trip distribution across all drivers to maximise fleet productivity

### 🏢 4.3 Customer Classification (RFM Analysis)

#### Interpretation
- Customers are classified based on three dimensions —Recency (how recently they ordered), Frequency (howoften they order) and Monetary value (revenue generated)
- A small group of high-frequency clients contributes disproportionately to total revenue
- Some clients order regularly but in low volumes, while others place large but infrequent orders
- RFM scoring reveals clear customer tiers that are not visible from trip data alone

#### Recommendations
- **Prioritise High-Value Clients** — Ensure top RFM-scored clients always receive timely deliveries and dedicated fleet allocation
- **Re-engage Low-Recency Clients** — Clients who have not ordered recently should be followed up to understand reasons and retain their business
